# M2 RL schema-2 reward overhead

## Question

What is the same-host throughput cost of the owner-selected dense engagement
reward relative to the accepted M2 sparse-reward baseline?

## Compared implementations

- Baseline: local commit `36297e5`, corresponding to the published M2
  checkpoint report.
- Candidate: `agent/lock-m2-rl-contract`, with seed-redacted observations and
  the optimized 1v1 engagement-potential fast path.

The state/tick implementation, action trace, environment count, compiler,
build preset, and benchmark executable are otherwise identical.

## Environment

- Linux 6.12.13 x86_64
- Intel Xeon Platinum 8573C virtualized lane, 9 single-threaded virtual cores
- GCC 13.3.0
- CMake `headless` preset
- 64 duel environments
- 32,768 rounds in the representative calibrated runs

## Method

Five baseline/candidate process pairs were interleaved. Each process reports
the median of seven internally calibrated samples and verifies exact
single/batch final-state equality. Process medians were then compared to reduce
the effect of shared-host noise.

## Result

| Version | Single-step env-ticks/s | Batched env-ticks/s |
|---|---:|---:|
| M2 baseline median | 15,171,380 | 15,611,715 |
| RL schema 2 median | 14,457,153 | 14,734,170 |
| Relative change | -4.71% | -5.62% |

One schema-2 process was a shared-host outlier near 9.3 million env-ticks/s;
the median excludes it. Every sample passed state equality.

## Interpretation

The observed cost is the deterministic reward calculation itself: two bounded
distance-potential evaluations per successful step. Seed redaction and schema
metadata do not add meaningful hot-path work. The implementation uses:

- one shared potential for both players in the common duel path;
- a constant right-shift scale rather than runtime division;
- no allocation, I/O, platform call, or additional canonical state;
- a four-player nearest-opponent path only for team matches.

The shaped reward is an explicit owner-selected feature and remains above
14 million environment-ticks/s on this host. M3 should store repeated samples
in the persistent performance database and establish a regression threshold;
later reward additions must be benchmarked against this schema-2 baseline.
